01 / About me

At the intersection of AI and biology

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Computer Science at Wayne State University, working with Dr. Suzan Arslanturk. My research combines machine learning, statistical genomics, graph representation learning, and explainable AI to understand the molecular drivers of cancer.

My dissertation, Genomic Biomarker Discovery for Cancer Prognosis and Precision Oncology, focuses on computational approaches that distinguish aggressive from less aggressive cancer, identify relevant somatic variants, and connect molecular discoveries to biological mechanisms and potential treatments.

I am passionate about developing rigorous, interpretable computational methods and bringing research ideas into the classroom. I anticipate completing my Ph.D. in May 2027.

Learning from Sparse Somatic Mutation Data

A graph-based framework combining a variational graph autoencoder and contrastive learning to capture patient similarity and identify influential variants using graph explainability techniques.

Where my research is headed
